Dynamic Programming

DSA · Memoisation

When working with arrays in DP, the key is to make decisions based on previous results, not by reordering the array.

Let's take Longest Increasing Subsequence (LIS) as an example.

We have an array:

arr = [35, 3, 2, 5, 3, 7, 101, 18]

We don't sort or reorder this.
We explore subsequences as we move forward.

At every index, we ask:
"What is the length of the LIS ending at this index?"

The maximum of this array is 4.
So, LIS = 4  →  3, 5, 7, 101

Why is the LIS ending at index 7 equal to 3?

Take a moment. Try to think it through before looking below.

At index 7, the value is 18. We look back for all elements smaller than 18 and find the best chain ending before it. The longest is 2 → 5 → 7 (length 3), and 18 extends it to make a subsequence of length 3 — not 4, because 101 > 18 and breaks the order.
